# Disaster Preparedness

Generate personalised disaster preparedness plans based on location-specific climate hazards and household characteristics.

## When to Use

- Homeowner wants to prepare for natural disasters in their area
- Community resilience planning for climate-related events
- Insurance review requiring documented preparedness measures
- TCFD physical risk adaptation strategy for residential assets

## Methodology

### Hazard Assessment

Combines multiple peril assessments to identify the most relevant preparedness actions:

1. **Flood** — FEMA flood zone, historical flood frequency, elevation analysis
2. **Wildfire** — WUI proximity, vegetation density, defensible space assessment
3. **Hurricane** — Historical storm tracks, wind zone classification, storm surge exposure
4. **Extreme Heat** — Heat wave frequency trends, urban heat island effects
5. **Drought** — Water stress indicators, groundwater dependency

### Preparedness Plan Components

- Emergency supply checklist (72-hour kit)
- Evacuation route planning with real-time hazard overlays
- Home hardening recommendations ranked by cost-effectiveness
- Insurance coverage gap analysis
- Communication plan template

## Output

Structured preparedness report with prioritised actions, estimated costs, and links to local emergency management resources.

## Active Emergency Escalation Protocol

**If a user indicates they are in an active emergency right now, apply this protocol before any other response:**

1. **Life safety first**: If there is any risk to life, reply immediately:
   > "If you are in immediate danger, call **911** now. Do not wait."

2. **Official instructions override everything**: Direct the user to:
   - Local emergency management website (search "[county name] emergency management")
   - NOAA Weather alerts: weather.gov
   - FEMA disaster assistance: disasterassistance.gov or 1-800-621-FEMA (3362)
   - Red Cross shelter finder: redcross.org/find-shelter

3. **Evacuation orders**: If the user asks about evacuation orders, say:
   > "I do not have access to live evacuation orders. Check your local emergency management website or call 211 for shelter locations."

4. **Do not provide operational emergency guidance** (e.g., "it is safe to stay") that requires real-time situational awareness the agent does not have. Clearly say: "I do not have real-time emergency data."

5. **Post-emergency**: Once the user is safe, this skill can help with damage documentation, insurance claim intake, and recovery planning.
